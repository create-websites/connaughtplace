Chandni Chowk

Constructed in 1650 by Princess Jahanara, the area known as 'Moonlight Square' featured a reflecting pool and canal. Originally famous for its silver vendors, the space evolved into niche markets before transforming into a vibrant wholesale market that serves as a key hub in Asia.

Khari Baoli

Since 1850, it has held the title of Asia's largest spice center. The area is filled with the potent scents of chilies, turmeric, and cardamom, providing visitors with one of Old Delhi's most enchanting sensory experiences.

Chawri Bazaar

Established in 1840, Delhi's first wholesale market specialized in hardware before expanding to become a central hub for brass, copper, and paper products, particularly wedding stationery.

Old vs. New: A Tale of Two Cities

Clash of Ideologies

Shahjahanabad united ruler and city through public display, while Lutyens' Delhi emphasized separation and control, distancing the administrative space from the old city. The deliberate positioning of 'New' Delhi as modern and cutting-edge rendered Mughal Delhi as antiquated and historical.

The Conservation Conundrum

Old Delhi struggles with overcrowding and illegal construction, creating a conflict between conservation and functionality. Traditional *havelis* are repurposed as storage facilities, and attempts to improve areas like Chandni Chowk from the top down often fail to consider the district's vibrant character, ultimately harming local businesses.
