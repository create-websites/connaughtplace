The Short Version

Not the cheapest market, not the grandest mall

Connaught Place was planned as a British imperial business district and completed in 1933, two years after New Delhi's inauguration. Architect Robert Tor Russell modelled it loosely on the Royal Crescent in Bath but stretched into a full circle of two-storey commercial blocks rather than a residential half-moon.

Nearly a century on, CP still works because it holds several cities at once: colonnaded colonial calm, bargain markets underground and around the edges, heritage restaurants that haven't changed their recipes in decades, and global brands next door to hand-spun khadi. This guide breaks it into four walks start anywhere on the circle.

Before You Go

Getting there, timing, and finding your way round the circle

Arrive by Metro

Rajiv Chowk sits directly under CP and links the Blue and Yellow lines, with exits into different blocks. Note the block letter on the exit sign to orient yourself. Parking is limited the Metro or a cab is easiest.

Best Time

Late morning to early evening suits shopping and cafés; evenings turn lively with diners and nightlife. Weekdays beat weekends for crowds, and in summer it's best to stroll early or after sunset.

Navigating

Blocks A–F form the Inner Circle around Central Park; G–N form the larger Outer Circle. Move block by block rather than wandering, wear comfortable shoes the inner loop is roughly 1.5 km and look up at the colonnade.

Questions travellers ask

FAQ

What is Connaught Place known for?

CP is Delhi's colonial-era commercial heart white Georgian colonnades in two concentric circles, a mix of global brands and bargain markets, and heritage restaurants and bakeries such as United Coffee House and Wenger's. It's as much a place to walk and people-watch as a place to buy something.

Why is it also called Rajiv Chowk?

The area was officially renamed Rajiv Chowk in 1995, after former Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi. The new name mostly stuck to the Metro station most Delhiites still say Connaught Place, or simply CP.

How do I reach Connaught Place?

The Metro is easiest: Rajiv Chowk station is directly beneath CP and connects the Blue and Yellow lines, with exits into several blocks. It's about 2 km from New Delhi Railway Station, and autos and cabs are widely available. Private parking fills up fast on busy days.

When is the best time to visit?

Late morning to early evening works well for shopping and café visits; from about 7 PM the restaurants and bars come alive. Weekdays are calmer than weekends. In Delhi's hot months, stroll in the morning or after sunset rather than at midday.

Where should I shop in and around CP?

The Inner and Outer Circles hold branded and lifestyle stores with fixed prices; Palika Bazaar underneath is a bargain-hunting maze; and nearby Janpath Market is the spot for handicrafts, jewellery, clothes and souvenirs, where haggling is expected.

Which traditional restaurants are worth it?

Try the long-standing names: United Coffee House (1942) for atmosphere, Wenger's bakery for patties and pastries, Kake Da Hotel for hearty Punjabi food, and heritage rooms like Embassy and Kwality plus the no-frills, nostalgic Indian Coffee House.
